# Language models answer around their own maps

The finding belongs to a well-established line of "decodable but not used" work, and it extends that line into territory none of the checked papers covers. Amnesic probing ([Elazar et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00995)) is the direct template, including the equal-rank random-direction baseline. In BERT it found phrase markers that were highly decodable yet whose erasure did not hurt the model, with **probe accuracy uncorrelated with task importance (Spearman 0.085, p = 0.87)**. What is new here is the object of study (a *composed, in-context 2-D world model*), the scale (12B/27B decoder LLMs), the strength of the null (copying, steering and every-token erasure across layers 12–30 all match equal-rank random erasure), and the pairing of that null with a positive causal localization elsewhere (a 2-D DAS subspace over the stated sentence, 72%/83% IIA vs 0% random). The two closest prior claims point the other way. [Tehenan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02996) report 74.3% steering success on text-described layouts but, as far as the notes could tell, ran no random-direction control. [David & Fomin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24425) report that in-context geometry is "causally used, not a probe correlate" in larger Gemma and Qwen models. Both are non-peer-reviewed preprints, and both test something different from probe directions of a composed 2-D map. The positive result fits the binding and "lookback" literature. LMs attach compact low-rank IDs to stated facts and retrieve them by attention at query time ([Feng & Steinhardt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191); [Prakash et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14685); [Gur-Arieh et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)), and in-context two-hop reasoning runs by sequential retrieval ([Guo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13913)). The switch-like behaviour of the DAS subspace has a cognitive precedent in the categorical-vs-coordinate distinction ([Kosslyn et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.2.562)). It also echoes the imagery debate's oldest result: metric scanning effects disappear when the task does not force use of the image ([Kosslyn, Ball & Reiser 1978](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.4.1.47)). Reviewers will press four threats: nonlinear leakage after linear erasure, self-repair, DAS interpretability illusions, and the scale argument from David & Fomin. The highest-value next steps are to finish P7 with windowed, edge-specific knockouts, to close the erasure loopholes (LEACE, post-erasure nonlinear probes, DAS on the composed variable itself), and to run the standard illusion controls on the DAS subspace.

**How to read the citations.** Every citation below comes from the research notes. Each table gives the peer-review status. "Preprint" means not peer-reviewed as far as the notes could establish. Every **UNVERIFIED** marker from the notes is carried over. Each one flags a venue, author list, detail or content claim that the note-takers did not check against a source page. Where two notes disagreed about a venue, both statuses are reported. Tags: **SUPPORTS** (backs our reading), **CONTRASTS** (found the opposite, or used weaker controls), **GAP** (we add what it lacks), **METHOD** (shapes our design), **CAUTION** (a limitation or objection it raises).

## 1. Decodability has never implied use, and the same erasure design has detected use before

### Amnesic probing is the direct precedent, and LEACE shows the design has teeth

The study replicates the logic of amnesic probing almost exactly: INLP erasure, an equal-rank random-direction baseline, and a downstream-accuracy readout. Elazar et al. found that removing dependency labels or fine-grained POS from BERT dropped masked-LM accuracy by **87.0 and 81.8 points**, while removing phrase start/end markers *raised* it slightly (**+0.21, +0.32**). They read this as "this information is simply not being used at this part of the model" ([Elazar et al. 2021, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00995v3)). The composed map in Gemma behaves like their phrase markers. The answer to "is our null just a weak method?" comes from [LEACE (Belrose et al. 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819). Their "concept scrubbing" applies the same every-layer-vs-random-subspace design to autoregressive LMs. Random erasure "has little to no effect", while scrubbing POS "induces a large increase in perplexity across all models". The design detects reliance when reliance exists. That makes our null informative rather than a symptom of an insensitive test.

Usage-based probing ([Lasri et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08831)) and counterfactual "push" interventions ([AlterRep, Ravfogel et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06965); [Tucker et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14002)) turned this into a methodology. In their cases the target property *was* used. That contrast strengthens our result: the analogous push interventions (map copying and probe-direction steering) succeeded for them and failed for us. Lasri et al. also found *separate* number encodings at nouns and at verbs. That precedent supports our finding that the used code sits at a different location (the stating sentence) from the decodable one (the composed map).

### What linear erasure cannot rule out

The erasure literature names exactly the loopholes a reviewer will raise. INLP guarantees only that *linear* classifiers fail ([Ravfogel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07667)), and it is not rank-optimal ([R-LACE, Ravfogel et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12091)). Kernelized erasure does not transfer across nonlinear adversaries, and full erasure of a nonlinearly encoded concept "remains an open problem" ([Ravfogel, Vargas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12191)). A multiclass log-linear head, which the LM unembedding is, "can be constructed that indirectly recovers the concept in some cases" ([Ravfogel, Goldberg & Cotterell 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10012)). Probe-based removal can strip non-concept features the probe happened to use ([Kumar et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04153)). [Canby et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15510) show a completeness–selectivity trade-off: nullifying methods (INLP, RLACE) are less complete than counterfactual ones, and "nonlinear interventions are almost always more reliable than linear interventions." The decodability of nonlinear distance (**0.62 vs −0.05 for the additive baseline**) makes nonlinear leftovers plausible, so this is the most serious objection to the null.

The design already answers most of it. Leakage cannot explain why *adding* a different map (copy-patching, steering) changes nothing. Canby et al. rate counterfactual interventions of that kind as more complete. The "destroyed task features" failure mode of Kumar et al. is ruled out because erasure costs no more than random. The remaining gap is that the paper should report *completeness* directly: linear and nonlinear probe R² after erasure.

### Probing critiques fix the framing: R² measures presence

From 2019 to 2022 the literature settled that probe accuracy reflects probe capacity ([Hewitt & Liang 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368); [Voita & Titov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)). Information is trivially "present" by the data-processing inequality ([Pimentel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03061)). "Usable" in V-information means usable *by the probe* ([Hewitt et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09234)). Correlational probing cannot establish use ([Belinkov 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12452); [Ravichander et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00719)). A 2026 Gemma-family preprint restates the point with SAEs. Probe-weighted and behaviour-driving features overlap only **~12% (ρ = 0.10)**, and ablating probe-only features flips **6%** of outputs vs **1%** for random features ([Tiwari et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18080)). Our result is stronger in one respect: the probe directions had *no* effect beyond random. The R² of 0.74 should therefore be framed as presence, reported with a selectivity control, and all "use" claims should rest on the interventions.

### Interpretability illusions threaten the positive DAS result, not the null

DAS ([Geiger et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997); [Geiger et al. 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536); [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08809)) optimizes interchange-intervention accuracy (IIA). RAVEL shows that such causally optimized subspaces beat probe-derived ones at isolating features that *control* behaviour ([Huang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17700)), which matches our probe-vs-DAS contrast. Two critiques apply to the DAS side. [Makelov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030) show that a subspace patch can work by "activating a dormant parallel pathway" that is causally disconnected from outputs in clean runs. [Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12631) counter that these illusions are "artifacts of their training and evaluation paradigms". [Sutter et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08802) prove that unrestricted alignment maps can map "any neural network … to any algorithm", reaching 100% IIA on randomly initialised models. Our 2-D *linear* DAS sits on the defensible side of that dilemma. It still needs a random-init sanity check and evidence that the subspace varies naturally in clean runs. The illusion argument concerns false *positives*; it does not touch the main negative claim about probe directions. That claim is threatened by leakage and compensation instead. Compensation is real. Backup name movers ([Wang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593)), the Hydra effect ([McGrath et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771)) and self-repair through LayerNorm and anti-erasure neurons ([Rushing & Nanda 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15390)) all show that a null ablation can reflect redundancy. Erasure at every token across 19 layers, combined with null *additive* interventions, leaves little room for that explanation. Finally, [Hase et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213) show that where information is localized and where editing works come apart, which is our pattern in a new domain.

## 2. World models are used when the task leaves no shortcut, and mostly untested when it does

### Single-task models use their boards; general LLMs reading text are a different regime

The canonical "world model" results come from small transformers trained on one closed domain, and there the state is causally used. Othello-GPT's board is decodable, and editing it changes move predictions ([Li et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382)). In the right "mine/theirs" basis it is linear and steerable by vector arithmetic ([Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941); [Hazineh et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07582)). A chess GPT's board edits and a skill vector shift play, raising win rate **up to 2.6x** ([Karvonen 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15498)). Maze transformers' SAE world-model features are causal, though "it is easier to activate features than to suppress them" ([Spies et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11867)). Grid-world GPT-2 shows when a map is used at all ([Baumgartner et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13371)). A random-walk model builds a self-sufficient Cartesian-like map. A goal-directed shortest-path model keeps relying on explicit direction tokens at every layer. Across these papers a used map appears when predicting the next token *requires* integrating a path. When relations are stated and can be re-read, models keep a path- or token-dependent strategy. A pretrained LLM reading "C is 2 east of M" belongs to the second regime, which predicts our result. One methodological warning from the Othello line is that linearity, and therefore steerability, depends on the basis. A probe in absolute (x, y) coordinates could miss a referent-relative frame.

### Parametric geography is decodable; whether it is used remains open

[Gurnee & Tegmark 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02207) set the probe-first narrative, finding linear space and time representations in Llama-2 and calling for further investigation. The only causal follow-up in the notes is small-scale: interventions on DeBERTa and GPT-Neo spatial representations changed geospatial predictions ([Chen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16257)). Decodability comes cheaply. Geography is decodable in tiny models ([Godey et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19406)), and static word embeddings reach **R² 0.42–0.59** on Gurnee & Tegmark's own datasets ([Barenholtz 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04317)). [Vafa et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03689) show models that pass standard world-model diagnostics while their implied map is incoherent. [Vafa et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06952) show orbital-trajectory models learning task-specific heuristics instead of Newtonian mechanics. Both fit "the map is there but the computation runs elsewhere."

### Spatial maps from text: the two counterpoints and the convergent preprints

The closest prior claim is [Tehenan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02996). On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, PCA-derived spatial directions injected at the final token yield a **74.3% success rate (446/600; 95% CI 70.8–77.8)**, scored by lexical match on the named direction. The extracted text contains no random-direction control and no erasure test. Their intervention also pushes the *answer-relation* direction at the answer position, which can change the output word without any map being read. That is consistent with our finding that a switch-like code, not the composed map, controls answers. The most important counterpoint is [David & Fomin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24425). In Gemma and Qwen, in-context geometry (cycles, trees) dominates pretrained priors, and activation patching shows "the map is causally used, not a probe correlate", but only in larger models (named as up to "Gemma-31B and Qwen-27B"; these names come from the preprint and were not checked against a model card). Their patching swaps whole *entity* activations, which is the analogue of our source-sentence DAS, not of probe directions on a composed 2-D map. On that reading, their result is closer to ours than to a rebuttal.

Several 2026 preprints converge on our side, though the notes checked only their abstracts. [An et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26323) find spatial information that "can causally influence behavior" but is "transient, fragmented across task families, and weakly integrated into final predictions". In 14 VLMs, [Liao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31257) find spatial answers decodable at 73–97% and steerable, yet depth is "deployed with the wrong sign". [Nedungadi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30751) find a decodable layout plan that "reflects only the layout implied by the prompt" while generation tracks the evolving state. Behavioural work agrees that LLMs rarely consult a cumulative map. CogEval finds hallucinated trajectories and loops ([Momennejad et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15129)). Gemini-2.5-Flash scores **80–86% with tokenized adjacency but 16–34% with visual grids**, and models "treat each question independently" ([Li et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10690)). Externalizing a map through Visualization-of-Thought prompting helps substantially ([Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03622)). Reading VoT's gains as evidence that the latent map is not used by default is our inference, not the authors' claim. The standard text spatial benchmarks (bAbI 17/19, StepGame, SpartQA, SpaRTUN) have **no mechanistic or probing study** in a modern LLM that the notes could find (moderate confidence; the search was not exhaustive).

## 3. In-context composition runs on query-time retrieval of bound facts

### Facts in weights get a precomputed bridge; facts in context get re-read

The multi-hop literature splits cleanly by where the facts live, and that split is the strongest mechanistic support for the positive result. For facts stored in weights, LLMs often resolve the bridge entity early and transport it. [Biran et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12775) used attention knockout over 7-layer windows and found bridge information passing from the first-hop token to the last token in **78–97% of correct cases**, mostly in middle layers (mean layer ~12.8/32 in LLaMA 2 7B, ~29.1/80 in LLaMA 3 70B). Back-patching fixed **32–66%** of failures. The bridge pathway is unreliable, though. Hop 1 is strong, while hop 2 has only moderate evidence and does not scale ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16837)). Composability is ~80% for country bridges but ~5% for year bridges ([Yang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16679)), which matters for numeric offsets. Later-hop answers can become decodable *before* bridges ([Liu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03542)), a direct warning that layer-wise decodability of an intermediate does not show it is used. Direct shortcut edges coexist with genuine two-step routes ([Lindsey et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)).

For facts in context, the picture is sequential retrieval at query time. A small transformer trained on in-context two-hop develops a "structured sequential query mechanism" that first retrieves the bridge and then the answer, while pretrained LLMs guess among candidates when distractors are present ([Guo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13913)). Arithmetic operands are moved to the final token by attention and then combined by MLPs ([Stolfo et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15054)), which is the nearest analogue to composing offset₁ + offset₂. The notes found **no mechanistic study of composing in-context numeric or spatial offsets** in a pretrained LLM. They also found no "copy a word-identical sentence from a run where the parent moved" transfer test like ours.

### Binding IDs and lookbacks give the positive result a mechanism

The binding literature describes the same pattern as our DAS result: compact codes written at the stated item and dereferenced by attention at query time. Binding-ID vectors attach entities to attributes and "form a continuous subspace" ([Feng & Steinhardt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191)). A low-rank Ordering-ID direction causally changes which attribute is bound ([Dai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05448)). Entity tracking follows the *position* of the correct entity ([Prakash et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811)). Belief tracking uses a two-stage "lookback": a binding lookback retrieves an Ordering ID (the pointer), then an answer lookback retrieves the state token (the payload). Information is "recalled when it becomes necessary" ([Prakash et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14685)). Retrieval mixes positional, lexical and reflexive pointers; a causal model combining all three matches next-token distributions at **95%** across 9 models and 10 tasks ([Gur-Arieh et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)). Propositional probes decode a faithful world state even when the model outputs something unfaithful ([Feng, Russell & Steinhardt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19501)). That is "decodable but unused" in a non-spatial domain.

Our switch-like behaviour fits this pointer reading, though this is our inference, not something the notes report. If the 2-D DAS subspace carries an *address* (which stated sentence or value to fetch) rather than a coordinate, three things follow. Interpolating halfway between two addresses should yield one fetch or the other, not an average. The code need not be linear in the stated offset. Writing in a value derived from the offset should move answers only partially (27%). Toy models allow both architectures: stored intermediates at selected positions ([Brinkmann et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11917)) and dynamic dereferencing through an addressable residual stream ([Wu, Geiger & Millière 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20896)). Gemma's composite looks like Brinkmann-style storage at the object list, but it behaves as epiphenomenal. One contrast deserves a direct test. In transitive in-context tasks, [Hong et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16975) find low-dimensional subspaces whose geometry *mirrors* the numeric parameterization, a dial rather than a switch.

### What this means for P7

The literature supplies a ready protocol and two expectations. Biran-style knockout blocks the source→query edge over sliding windows. Geva-style edge interventions separate relation flow from subject flow ([Geva et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)). Path patching separates the route through the question tokens from direct reads by the final token ([Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05969)). Gur-Arieh et al. and Lindsey et al. both predict *partial compensation* from redundant routes. P7 should therefore block attention to *all* tokens of M's sentence (positional, lexical and reflexive routes) and report effects cumulated across windows. The lookback account predicts that blocking question tokens from M's sentence should hurt answers about C, because composition happens at query time by re-reading M. Blocking C's own sentence should hurt too. Blocking the object list should cost little if the composite stored there is unused.

## 4. Cognitive science already distinguishes a categorical switch from a metric map

### Categorical and coordinate codes are separable, and separation helps

Kosslyn's theory holds that vision computes categorical relations (left/right, above/below, on/off) separately from coordinate (metric) relations ([Kosslyn 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.2.148)). Divided-visual-field experiments supported the split, and "categorical representations developed with practice" ([Kosslyn et al. 1989](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.15.4.723)). The 1992 connectionist simulations are the most transferable result: "networks that were split so that different hidden units contributed to each type of judgment performed better than unsplit networks" ([Kosslyn et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.18.2.562)). That gives a principled reason to expect a categorical answer code to sit apart from a metric map. Lesion evidence shows a double dissociation in **60 stroke patients** ([Laeng 1994](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.1994.6.3.189)). The hemispheric mapping is only partly supported ([Jager & Postma 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0028-3932(02)00086-6)), so borrow the representational distinction and leave the anatomy aside.

Human data also mark where the LM *differs*. Huttenlocher's Category Adjustment model stores locations as a fine-grain value plus a category and *blends* them, biasing estimates toward prototypes and truncating at category boundaries ([Huttenlocher et al. 1991](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.98.3.352)). That is a dial pulled toward a category. Our halfway interpolation gives no in-between answers, so the LM code is a purer switch than the human one. Truncation at category boundaries is the nearest human analogue. Stevens & Coupe's hierarchical distortions (Reno judged east of San Diego) are the best human case of answering from a categorical code while metric knowledge exists ([Stevens & Coupe 1978](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(78)90006-3)). The notes summarize that paper's content from background knowledge.

### Readers build maps from text but answer stated relations from the text

The best human parallel to "map built but not used" is [Taylor & Tversky 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(92)90014-O). Readers drew very accurate maps from route or survey descriptions, yet answered verbatim questions faster and more accurately, "suggesting that verbatim questions are verified against a representation of the text." Only inference questions were answered against the situation model. This predicts a sharp LM test. The switch code should govern *stated* relations, while the coordinate map would be recruited, if at all, only for *unstated* inferences. Franklin & Tversky found retrieval from text-derived models organized by categorical body axes rather than metric rotation ([Franklin & Tversky 1990](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.119.1.63)). Under indeterminacy, people build a single preferred model ([Ragni & Knauff 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032460); [Goodwin & Johnson-Laird 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.112.2.468)), another discrete default. The counter-case is [Morrow, Greenspan & Bower 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(87)90122-7). Accessibility fell with distance from the protagonist, so in humans the situation model's metric content *does* shape behaviour, and the design transfers to LMs as a probe.

### The imagery debate is the original "decodable ≠ used" argument

[Kosslyn, Ball & Reiser 1978](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.4.1.47) showed scanning time growing with distance across an imagined map. They also reported that "when subjects were not asked to base all judgments on examination of their images, the distance … did not affect reaction times." Even the founding pro-imagery paper shows a metric map that exists and is not used by default. Pylyshyn's tacit-knowledge critique ([1973](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0034650); [2002](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X02000043)) is in substance a causal-use objection: metric-looking behaviour does not show that a metric format does the work. Imagery can even impede relational reasoning ([Knauff & Johnson-Laird 2002](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03194937)). Aphantasia supplies natural ablations. Patient MX lost imagery but performed normally ([Zeman et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2009.08.024)). Aphantasics show no imagery-based rivalry priming yet above-average spatial imagery scores ([Keogh & Pearson 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2017.10.012)). **61 aphantasics** drew impaired object detail but intact spatial layout ([Bainbridge et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2020.11.014)). Group differences showed in response time, not accuracy ([Pounder et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2021.12.010)). Aphantasia concerns *depictive* imagery, so the analogy is "depictive format not needed", not "no map at all".

Finally, Behrens et al. define a cognitive map *functionally*, as structure that supports flexible inference ([Behrens et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.10.002)). Under that definition Gemma has a *geometric* map that fails as a *functional* one, which is a useful, citable distinction. TEM factorizes structure from binding ([Whittington et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024)), and transformers with recurrent position encodings reproduce place and grid codes ([Whittington, Warren & Behrens 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04035)). Together they suggest a mechanistic reading: the switch is a binding/lookup code, and the map is a structural basis recruited only for novel inference.

## 5. Novelty assessment: a controlled null plus a localization, on a thin and mostly preprint frontier

The genuinely new piece is the combination. None of the notes found an erasure study in which a concept was highly decodable (R² > 0.7) in a >10B decoder LLM, erased at every token across many layers, and the effect matched random. None found an erasure-vs-random test on text-described layouts at ≥10B, and none found a mechanistic study of composing in-context spatial or numeric offsets. Each absence claim rests on a search the notes call moderately confident, not exhaustive. Pairing the null with a positive 2-D DAS localization at the stating sentence, plus its switch-not-dial characterization, goes beyond the amnesic-probing template, which stopped at the null. Each component has a precedent, though. "Decodable but unused" is a 2021 result ([Elazar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00995)), and propositional probes already showed faithful-but-unused world states ([Feng, Russell & Steinhardt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19501)). Query-time retrieval of bound facts is a 2024–2026 consensus ([Prakash et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14685); [Gur-Arieh et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)). The honest framing is therefore *"LLMs build a decodable composed map but answer by re-reading per-sentence codes"*. That is a new, well-controlled instance of an established phenomenon in a domain where the field's narrative ("LLMs have spatial world models") runs the other way. It is not a new phenomenon.

The counterpoints need direct engagement. **David & Fomin 2026** is the strongest. It shows Gemma-family in-context geometry causally used, with the effect emerging at larger scale. If our erasure null was run only at 12B, a reviewer will invoke their scale argument. If it holds at 27B, it overlaps the scale range they describe. Their entity-level patching is the analogue of our source-sentence DAS, not of composed-map probe directions, so the two results are arguably compatible. The point still has to be argued explicitly. **Tehenan et al. 2025** claims use through steering at 74.3%, but the extracted text has no random-direction control, and the intervention targets the answer-relation direction at the final token. Our finding offers a mechanism for why such steering works without a map. **Tiwari et al. 2026** and **An et al. 2026** are convergent but shift the priority picture: "decodable ≠ causal" in Gemma-family models and "weakly integrated" spatial representations are already in 2026 preprints. The novelty claim should therefore rest on the controls and the localization, not on the bare dissociation.

On peer-review status: the convergent spatial evidence leans on **non-peer-reviewed preprints**, namely Tehenan 2025, An 2026, Liao 2026, Nedungadi 2026, David & Fomin 2026, Tiwari 2026, Barenholtz 2026, Chen 2023, Li 2026 (mazes) and Xia 2025, and the notes checked only abstracts for the 2026 ones. Several mechanistic anchors are also preprints or have UNVERIFIED venues: Guo 2025, Yu 2025, Balesni 2024/25, Hong 2025, Hazineh 2023, Goldowsky-Dill 2023, Spies 2024, Ivanitskiy 2023, Prakash 2025 (venue UNVERIFIED), Dai 2024 (venue UNVERIFIED), McGrath 2023 (venue UNVERIFIED), Guerner 2023 (venue UNVERIFIED), and Lindsey 2025 (Transformer Circuits Thread). The core methodological lineage (Elazar, LEACE, INLP/RLACE, Makelov, Sutter, RAVEL, Feng & Steinhardt, Prakash 2024, Gur-Arieh, Biran) is peer-reviewed.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the result *is*. It reads less as "LLMs lack spatial world models" and more as a case of two well-documented mechanisms meeting. Transformers build geometry cheaply wherever the input implies it: Othello boards, in-context grids, and our composed map. They then answer through whatever retrieval route the input makes available, and stated relations make a binding-and-lookback route available. Both the single-task world-model papers and the grid-world GPT-2 results predict that a map is used only when no such route exists. That turns experiment 5 from an add-on into the decisive test. If Gemma ignores the map even when the answer cannot be re-read from any stated sentence, the "geometric but not functional map" claim becomes much stronger than any current paper makes it.

The switch-not-dial behaviour is the most original part of the result, and the least anchored in prior work. Binding IDs are described as continuous subspaces, numeric in-context parameters as dial-like geometry, and human spatial memory as a category-weighted blend. An LM code that snaps between stated values, resists linear write-in, and sits at the source sentence therefore behaves more like an address than a coordinate. Showing that directly (experiment 4) would tie spatial reasoning to the pointer mechanisms of entity tracking. It would also suggest that the field's probe-based "spatial world model" claims, including the 2025–2026 preprints, have often been measuring the by-product rather than the path.
